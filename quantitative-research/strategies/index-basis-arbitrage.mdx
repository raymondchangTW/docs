## 1. Fair value

With discrete dividends, cost-of-carry fair value of an index future is

`F* = (S - PV(D)) x (1 + r x d / 365)`

where S is spot, D is the dividends expected before expiry, r is the funding rate, and d is days to expiry. Use the continuously compounded form if the desk convention requires it.

**Worked example (verified numerically).** S = 20,000, dividends = 60 points, r = 4%, d = 30 days, F = 20,040.

- Fair value = (20,000 - 60) x (1 + 0.04 x 30/365) = 20,005.56.
- Mispricing = 20,040 - 20,005.56 = 34.4 points.
- Implied financing rate = (F / (S - D) - 1) x 365 / d = 6.10%.

The implied financing rate is the cleaner quantity to monitor: it can be compared directly with the desk's funding curve and with other expiries.

## 2. Cost band

A trade is only attractive when |F - F\*| exceeds the all-in round-trip cost:

- Futures spread and fees, basket crossing cost, stamp duty or transaction levies where applicable.
- Borrow cost if the cash leg is short.
- Funding spread over the risk-free rate.
- Dividend uncertainty (ex-date and amount) and index-weight rebalancing risk.
- Tracking error of the replicating basket (full replication vs sampled).

Band asymmetry is normal: short-stock legs are costlier than long-stock legs.

## 3. Modelling the basis

1. Convert futures prices into implied financing (or implied dividend) so that different expiries are comparable.
2. Fit a term structure of implied financing. Deviations of a single expiry from the curve are calendar-spread signals.
3. Model the residual as mean-reverting with a half-life estimated out of sample. See [stat arb](/quantitative-research/strategies/stat-arb-pairs) for the estimator.
4. Treat dividend forecasts as a separate model: the implied dividend is the main source of apparent mispricing around the dividend season.

## 4. Execution structure

- **Basket vs futures** requires simultaneous execution; leg risk is the dominant risk.
- **Closing-auction execution** lets the cash leg trade at the official close, which matters for index-tracking flows. See [auction mechanisms](/quantitative-research/microstructure/auction-mechanisms).
- **Roll trades** (expiry to expiry) carry less cash-leg risk but depend on the calendar spread.

## 5. Risks

- Dividend surprise or ex-date shift.
- Index rebalancing and constituent changes.
- Borrow recall.
- Margin and funding changes on the futures leg.
- Settlement price definition (average of final-day quotes vs closing price).
