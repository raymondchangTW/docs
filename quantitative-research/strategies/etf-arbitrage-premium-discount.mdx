## 1. Definitions

- **Premium/discount (bps)** = (P / NAV - 1) x 10,000, where P is the ETF price and NAV is the reference value at the same timestamp.
- **iNAV** (intraday indicative NAV) is the basket value computed from live or last-known constituent prices, converted to ETF currency. It is an estimate, not a tradable price.
- Example: P = 20.04, NAV = 20.00 gives a premium of 20.0 bps.

## 2. Why a premium or discount can persist

The arbitrage band is set by the cost of the creation/redemption (C/R) loop, not by zero:

- Upper bound: premium cannot exceed the cost of buying the basket, creating, and selling the ETF (basket spread + C/R fee + financing/hedge cost + risk premium).
- Lower bound: symmetric for redemption, usually wider if constituents are hard to short or borrow.
- Inside the band, the premium is a noisy state variable, not an opportunity.

Band width drivers: constituent liquidity and spread, borrow availability, creation unit size, settlement cycle mismatch, FX hedge cost, and the cutoff time for C/R requests.

## 3. Stale pricing and non-synchronous markets

When the ETF trades while constituents are closed or illiquid (cross-time-zone ETFs, A-share and bond ETFs), measured premium reflects information arriving at the ETF first, not mispricing. Petajisto (2017) documents that premiums for international and fixed-income ETFs are larger and more persistent than for domestic equity ETFs, and that part of the gap is stale NAV.

Practical consequences:

- Measure premium against a **fair-value NAV** built from futures, ADRs, or factor models, not the last official close.
- Treat the iNAV update interval and its FX source as model inputs.
- Separate the **informational** premium (ETF leads) from the **liquidity** premium (ETF absorbs flow).
- Build the fair-value NAV by asset class and currency as described on [ETF fair value models](/quantitative-research/strategies/etf-fair-value-models) and [ETF fair value: multi-currency framework](/quantitative-research/strategies/etf-fair-value-multi-currency).

## 4. A simple model

Let x_t = log(P_t / FV_t) where FV is fair-value NAV. A common reduced form is a mean-reverting process with a no-trade band:

- Inside the band: dx = -kappa x dt + sigma dW.
- Outside the band: reversion is accelerated by the arbitrageur flow (regime-switching or threshold AR).

Estimate kappa and the band edges from tick data, using the same timestamp alignment for ETF quotes and basket prices. Report half-life = ln 2 / kappa. The result is only meaningful if the NAV timestamp is aligned with the quote timestamp.

## 5. Signals and filters used in practice

| Signal | Use | Main failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Premium z-score vs fair-value NAV | Entry | Stale NAV makes z-score look large |
| Premium vs C/R cost band | Execution decision | Band estimated with wrong borrow cost |
| Flow (creations/redemptions, AUM change) | Persistence | Reported with a lag |
| Constituent spread and depth | Capacity | Depth at top of book overstates size |

## 6. Hedging and P&L decomposition

A premium trade (sell ETF, buy basket) earns the premium minus: basket crossing cost, ETF crossing cost, financing, tracking error until C/R settles, and residual delta from non-hedged constituents. Decompose realized P&L into (a) premium capture, (b) hedge slippage, (c) financing and fees, (d) basis between futures hedge and basket. ## Points to verify

- HK ETP C/R cutoff times, minimum creation unit sizes, and cash-component rules per product.

- Petajisto's magnitudes are from US data; do not apply them to HK without local estimation.
