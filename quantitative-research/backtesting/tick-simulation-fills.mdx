## 1. Data requirements

- **Level of detail:** trades and top-of-book quotes support aggressive-order simulation. Passive-order simulation needs depth and, ideally, order-level events.
- **Timestamps:** keep exchange timestamp and local receive timestamp separately; the difference is your latency to the feed.
- **Sequencing:** ensure the sequence number is monotone and gaps are detected.
- **Phases:** include opening and closing auction phases and their rules ([auction mechanisms](/quantitative-research/microstructure/auction-mechanisms)).

## 2. Aggressive (marketable) orders

- Fill against displayed depth at the simulated arrival time (event time plus latency), walking the book.
- Use the book as of arrival, not as of decision; the book may have changed in the latency window.
- Add impact for orders above displayed depth, using a fitted impact curve ([market impact and TCA](/quantitative-research/microstructure/market-impact-tca)).

## 3. Passive orders

Three rule families, in increasing realism:

| Rule | Fill when | Bias |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Touch | Price reaches the limit | Strongly optimistic: ignores queue |
| Trade-through | A trade prints strictly through the limit price | Pessimistic: misses fills at the limit |
| Queue-aware | Cumulative volume at the price exceeds the shares ahead | Realistic if queue position is well estimated |

For queue-aware simulation, set the initial position from displayed depth at arrival, and decide a cancellation assumption (none ahead cancels, or a proportional share). Report results under at least a conservative and an optimistic assumption. See [queue position and fills](/quantitative-research/microstructure/queue-position-fill).

## 3.1 Adverse selection in simulation

A simulator that fills by random arrival will not reproduce adverse selection. Use real historical trades so that fills coincide with actual price moves, and measure P&L using the mid some interval after the fill.

## 4. Latency

Model three components: market data latency, strategy compute time, and order entry latency. Apply them as delays on events. Run the same strategy under several latency settings; the P&L-versus-latency curve shows how latency-sensitive the strategy is.

## 5. Own-order impact

Historical data does not contain your orders. Common approximations: reduce displayed depth by your own size after a fill, apply a decay impact model for aggressive orders, and cap participation as a fraction of the observed volume.

## 6. Validation against live

1. Run the simulator over a period with live fills.
2. Compare simulated and actual fill rates, fill times, and realised spread.
3. Adjust only the fill-model parameters (queue assumption, cancellation share), not the strategy.
4. Keep the live-versus-sim gap as a standing diagnostic.
