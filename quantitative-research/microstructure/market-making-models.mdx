## 1. The Avellaneda-Stoikov (A-S) model

Setup: mid price follows arithmetic Brownian motion with volatility sigma; the market maker has CARA utility with risk aversion gamma, holds inventory q, and trades until T. Fill intensity at distance delta from the mid is lambda(delta) = A x exp(-k x delta).

Two outputs:

- **Reservation price** r = s - q x gamma x sigma^2 x (T - t). Positive inventory lowers r, so the maker quotes lower to sell and discourages further buying.
- **Optimal total spread** = gamma x sigma^2 x (T - t) + (2 / gamma) x ln(1 + gamma / k). Quotes are placed symmetrically around r.

**Worked example (verified).** s = 100, gamma = 0.1, sigma = 2, T - t = 1, k = 1.5.

- Spread = 0.4 + 1.291 = 1.691.
- Inventory 0: reservation 100.0, bid 99.15, ask 100.85.
- Inventory +5: reservation 98.0, bid 97.15, ask 98.85. The whole quote shifts down by 2.0, i.e. by q x gamma x sigma^2 x tau.

## 2. What the model leaves out

- **Adverse selection:** the mid is exogenous and no trade is informative. In practice this is the dominant cost; add a flow-toxicity term or widen the spread where order-flow imbalance is high.
- **Fill intensity form:** the exponential form is an assumption. Estimate lambda(delta) from own fills, including queue position effects ([queue position and fills](/quantitative-research/microstructure/queue-position-fill)).
- **Discreteness:** continuous quotes ignore the tick size. Quotes must be rounded to the tick grid, which can dominate the optimal spread in large-tick stocks.
- **Constant sigma:** use a short-horizon vol estimate and a floor.
- **Terminal horizon:** the finite T produces a spread that shrinks toward the end; many implementations use an infinite-horizon variant or a rolling horizon.

## 3. Practical extensions

1. **Inventory limits and skew caps:** hard position bounds, with quote withdrawal on the side that would breach.
2. **Signal skew:** shift the reservation price by a short-horizon alpha (imbalance, hedge-instrument move).
3. **Hedging inventory:** for ETFs or options the inventory can be hedged with futures or baskets, so the relevant risk is the residual after hedge, not q itself.
4. **Obligation-aware quoting:** exchange requirements on spread, size, and uptime constrain the optimum; model them as constraints.
5. **Multi-asset:** inventory risk becomes a covariance term; skews depend on the whole portfolio.

## 4. Evaluating a quoting model

- Compare realised spread, fill rate, inventory distribution, and P&L variance against a baseline of fixed-width quotes.
- Run in a simulator that models queue position and adverse selection, otherwise A-S looks better than it is ([tick simulation and fills](/quantitative-research/backtesting/tick-simulation-fills)).
