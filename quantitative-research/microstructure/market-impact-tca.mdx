## 1. Impact models

- **Kyle (1985) linear model:** price change = lambda x signed net order flow. lambda falls as noise trading rises. It is an equilibrium coefficient of an information model, not automatically the causal impact of one trade.
- **Square-root law:** metaorder impact is roughly Y x sigma x sqrt(Q / V), where sigma is daily volatility, Q is the order size, V is daily volume, and Y is a constant of order one. The shape is widely reported across markets; Y must be calibrated.
- **Temporary vs permanent:** temporary impact decays after execution; permanent impact is the information content. Almgren and Chriss (2001) use linear permanent and temporary components.

**Illustration.** sigma = 2% daily, participation Q / V = 1%, Y = 1 (assumed): impact = 0.02 x sqrt(0.01) = 0.002 = 20 bps. Halving the order size reduces impact by a factor of sqrt(0.5), i.e. about 29%, not 50%.

## 2. Execution schedules

| Schedule | Rule | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TWAP | Equal slices over time | Ignores volume pattern |
| VWAP | Slices proportional to expected volume | Depends on volume forecast |
| POV | Fixed share of market volume | Duration is random |
| Implementation shortfall / arrival price | Front-loaded to limit timing risk | Higher impact |

**Almgren-Chriss.** Minimise expected cost plus lambda x variance. In the linear model the efficient frontier of strategies (lowest expected cost for a given risk) can be constructed explicitly, and the risk-neutral solution is a uniform (TWAP-like) schedule; more risk aversion front-loads. The same framework leads to liquidity-adjusted VaR.

## 3. TCA metrics

- **Implementation shortfall** = (execution price - decision price) x side, plus fees, and an opportunity cost term for unfilled shares.
- **Arrival slippage** vs mid at order arrival.
- **VWAP slippage** vs interval VWAP (can be gamed by trading with the benchmark).
- **Reversion**: mid move after completion; high reversion indicates temporary impact, low indicates information.
- **Spread capture** for passive fills, measured with post-fill mid ([queue position and fills](/quantitative-research/microstructure/queue-position-fill)).

## 4. Building a cost model

1. Collect parent order data: size, start/end, participation, symbol, venue, side.
2. Fit cost = a x spread/2 + b x sigma x (Q/V)^c on parent orders; test c near 0.5.
3. Check out of sample and by volatility regime.
4. Use the fitted function as the cost term in optimisers and as the fee in [backtests](/quantitative-research/backtesting/tick-simulation-fills).

## 5. Caveats

- Own-flow impact is confounded with signal: orders placed when alpha is strong move with the market regardless of impact.
- HK-specific: closing auction participation and the tick table both change the cost curve ([auction mechanisms](/quantitative-research/microstructure/auction-mechanisms), [tick size rules](/quantitative-research/microstructure/tick-size-rules)).
