## 1. Limit order book basics

- Orders rest at discrete price levels under **price-time priority**. Best bid and ask define the touch; depth is the size at each level.
- Quoted spread = ask - bid. Effective spread = 2 x |trade price - mid| for a trade. Realised spread = effective spread minus the price move over a later horizon, which isolates what the liquidity provider keeps after adverse selection.
- Mid and microprice: mid = (bid + ask) / 2. A common microprice weights by opposite-side size: (bid x ask_size + ask x bid_size) / (bid_size + ask_size).

## 2. Order flow signals

- **Queue imbalance** I = (bid_size - ask_size) / (bid_size + ask_size) at the touch. Positive imbalance tends to precede upward mid moves at short horizons; the relation is strongest in large-tick stocks and decays quickly.
- **Signed trade flow** (trade sign by tick rule or Lee-Ready) and its autocorrelation, which is long-memory in many markets.
- **Depth beyond the touch** matters for the cost of larger orders; top-of-book depth alone overstates liquidity.

## 3. Spread components

In a competitive market the spread compensates for three costs (Madhavan 2000; Biais, Glosten and Spatt 2005):

1. **Order processing** (fixed costs of providing quotes, fees).
2. **Inventory** (compensation for holding unbalanced positions; Stoll 1978).
3. **Adverse selection** (trading against better-informed counterparties; Glosten and Milgrom 1985, Kyle 1985).

Huang and Stoll (1997) construct a time-series model that identifies all three. Their empirical results point to a large order-processing component and smaller, but significant, adverse-selection and inventory components, and the components differ by trade size.

## 4. Estimation approaches

| Approach | Needs | Caveat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roll (1984) serial covariance | Trade prices only | Assumes no information effect; gives only an effective-spread estimate |
| Glosten-Harris (1988) | Signed trades | Trade classification error biases the split |
| Huang-Stoll (1997) | Signed trades, quotes | Sensitive to assumed order/trade relation |
| Realised-spread decomposition | Quotes, trades, horizon choice | Horizon is arbitrary; results depend on it |

## 5. Large tick vs small tick

When the minimum tick is large relative to the efficient spread, the spread is pinned at one tick, queues are long, and queue position carries value (see [queue position and fills](/quantitative-research/microstructure/queue-position-fill)). In small-tick stocks the spread is a free variable and quote competition occurs through price. See [tick size and exchange rules](/quantitative-research/microstructure/tick-size-rules).

## 6. Use in market making

The spread decomposition indicates how much of a quoted spread is capturable. Adverse-selection share, estimated per instrument and per time of day, feeds directly into the quote-skew parameters of [market-making models](/quantitative-research/microstructure/market-making-models).
