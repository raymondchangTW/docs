## 1. Why queue position matters

Under price-time priority, an order at the back of a long queue at the touch fills only after the orders ahead are executed or cancelled. Moallemi and Yuan model the value of a limit order as a function of its queue position, incorporating adverse selection and queueing dynamics, and find that queue value can be of the same order of magnitude as the bid-ask spread. Huang et al. (2019) reach a similar conclusion in an agent-based order book model. Consequence: in large-tick products, queue priority is an asset that has to be priced.

## 2. Components of a fill model

For a passive order at the touch, the fill depends on:

1. **Orders ahead** (position in queue at submission).
2. **Cancellations ahead** (reduce the queue without trading).
3. **Market orders arriving** at that side before the price moves away.
4. **Price move** (if the touch moves away, the order is no longer at the touch).

Simple benchmark: if volume at the touch trades at rate mu (shares per unit time) and cancellations ahead are ignored, expected time to fill is roughly (shares ahead) / mu. Including cancellations shortens the wait; including price moves lowers the probability of ever filling.

## 3. Adverse selection of a passive fill

Fills are not random: a bid fills more often when the price is about to fall. The conditional expected mid move after a fill is therefore negative for a buy order. Any estimate of passive-order profit must use the **post-fill** mid move, not the unconditional one. This is why a realised-spread calculation is a better backtest metric than quoted spread ([order book and spread](/quantitative-research/microstructure/order-book-spread)).

## 4. Modelling approaches

| Approach | Idea | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Poisson arrival of market orders | Fill time is exponential | Ignores clustering and cancellations |
| Queueing model with cancellations (fluid or diffusion limit) | Explicit queue dynamics | Needs calibrated arrival and cancel rates per level |
| Empirical hazard model | Fit time-to-fill on own order data | Needs enough own fills; regime dependent |
| Queue-reactive simulation | Rates depend on queue sizes | Calibration complexity |

## 5. Estimating queue position

- With a full order-by-order feed, reconstruct position exactly from order IDs.
- With only level-aggregated data, position is uncertain: assume a conservative rule (placed at the back) and an optimistic rule (pro-rata) and report both. A strategy that only works under the optimistic rule is not validated.

## 6. Backtesting implication

A naive backtest that fills a passive order when the price touches the limit price overstates fills and understates adverse selection. Use a fill model with an explicit queue assumption and a latency assumption. Detail on [tick simulation and fills](/quantitative-research/backtesting/tick-simulation-fills).
