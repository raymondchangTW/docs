This page describes the ETF primary market in general terms. It is not specific to one exchange. Market-specific rules, such as who may deal with the fund, cut-off times and tax treatment, belong on each market's own pages.

## Two markets for one product

An ETF trades in two places.

| Market | Who trades | Price set by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Secondary market | Investors and market makers on the exchange | Order book on the exchange |
| Primary market | Approved dealers dealing directly with the fund or its manager | The fund's net asset value (NAV) for the units created or redeemed |

Approved dealers go by different names depending on the market, for example participating dealers in Hong Kong. Dealers create units by delivering the required assets or cash to the fund, and redeem units by handing units back and receiving assets or cash.

## How the link to NAV works

Because an approved dealer can create or redeem at NAV, a gap between the exchange price and the NAV creates an opportunity. A dealer or market maker that buys the cheaper side and sells the dearer side can earn the difference. That activity pushes the exchange price back towards NAV. The Hong Kong regulator describes an efficient creation and redemption mechanism as the key to an effective arbitrage and pricing mechanism.

The size of the gap that is worth trading depends on costs and timing. These include trading costs, financing, hedging, any fees charged by the dealer or fund, and the time between the secondary trade and primary settlement.

## Features that differ between funds and markets

- **In kind or cash.** Creation and redemption can be made with the underlying assets or with cash. Which is allowed depends on the fund and on market rules.
- **Creation unit size.** Set by each fund in its documents.
- **Cut-off times and settlement cycles.** Set by the fund and by the settlement cycle of the market. A mismatch between secondary and primary settlement is a source of risk.
- **Suspension.** Primary market dealing can be suspended in specified circumstances, with effects on secondary market pricing.
- **Taxes and duties.** Often differ between secondary trading and primary dealing.
