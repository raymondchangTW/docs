This page covers the ETF primary market in Hong Kong: how units are created and redeemed with participating dealers (PDs), and how that mechanism supports ETF pricing and arbitrage. It is the link between ETF secondary market trading on HKEX and the ETF itself.

<Info>
**Status of this page.** Last checked on 15 November 2026. The SFC material is the June 2019 edition of its FAQs on exchange traded funds and listed funds. Creation unit sizes, PD agreements, cut-off times and fee schedules are set by each ETF's documents and are not covered here.
</Info>

Related pages: [ETF trading rules and costs](/equity-derivatives/hk-etf-trading-rules) and [ETF market maker rules](/equity-derivatives/hk-etf-market-maker-rules).

## How the mechanism works

An ETF has a secondary market, where investors trade on the exchange, and a primary market, where dealers create or redeem units with the fund. In Hong Kong the dealers are participating dealers (PDs). The SFC describes an efficient creation and redemption mechanism as the key to an effective arbitrage and pricing mechanism. That mechanism is what keeps an ETF trading close to its net asset value (NAV).

## Participating dealers

The SFC generally seeks to require PDs of SFC-authorised ETFs to process creation and redemption requests from third-party investors. It accepts exceptions in circumstances such as:

- Suspended dealing or suspended NAV determination.
- Adverse effects on the ETF.
- Suspended trading in index basket shares.
- Trading limits that stop the ETF from buying further investments.
- Conflicts with the PD's own compliance requirements.

The ETF's prospectus or constitutive documents should clearly disclose the creation and redemption procedures, any restrictions, the circumstances in which a request can be refused, and any PD fees or factors that reduce redemption proceeds.

## Manager responsibilities

- The manager must run the ETF in investors' interest, including closely monitoring its pricing and arbitrage mechanism.
- If the ETF trades at a substantial premium or discount to NAV, the manager should investigate and act promptly.

## Suspension of primary market dealing

- Under section 10.6 of the Code on Unit Trusts and Mutual Funds, the management company may suspend dealings in consultation with the trustee or custodian, having regard to holders' best interests.
- Under section 10.7, the manager must immediately notify the SFC when dealing in units ceases or is suspended. It must publish that fact immediately, and at least monthly during the suspension.
- The SFC's December 2020 guidance to the ETF industry asks managers to consider whether secondary market trading should also be suspended when primary market dealing is suspended.

## Stamp duty

Secondary market trading of ETPs is exempt from stamp duty. For primary market activity, SMMs and DSs may apply to waive stamp duty on stock transfers made in the course of creating and redeeming ETP units listed in Hong Kong. See the [ETF market maker rules](/equity-derivatives/hk-etf-market-maker-rules) page.

## In-kind and cash creation

The SFC FAQ used here does not address whether creation or redemption is in kind or in cash. For SFC-authorised spot virtual asset ETFs, the SFC allows both in-kind and cash subscription and redemption. HKEX announced a plan to digitise and automate the in-kind creation and redemption process for relevant ETPs from 2025, using a web-based platform that connects ETP participants with distributed ledger technology and smart contracts. The current status of that platform was not confirmed.

## T+1 implications

The move from T+2 to T+1 for the cash market raises a specific concern for ETPs. Law-firm commentary on the April 2026 consultation describes the ETP primary market as a central concern. One consultation respondent identified the multi-party sequence between the issuer, PDs, custodians, trustees and registrars as the main readiness risk, and asked HKEX for detailed cut-offs and end-to-end testing for cash and in-kind scenarios. HKEX's 16 October 2026 conclusions target T+1 in the second half of 2028, with the exact date still to be announced.

## Points to verify before relying on this page

1. The current SFC FAQs and any later guidance on PDs and primary market dealing.
2. Creation unit size, cut-off times and settlement cycles for the specific ETF, which are in its documents.
3. Whether HKEX's in-kind digitisation platform has launched and how it works.
4. How HKEX's T+1 conclusions treat the ETP primary market.
